Definition

Metabolism is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of living chemistry: how a cell takes in matter and energy, builds and repairs itself, and copies its DNA. Life is not a miracle but a sophisticated way for the universe to dissipate energy and manage entropy.

Metabolism is the hinge between the Physical and the Practical Realms: with it comes survival, the first norm.

Example

In 1953 Stanley Miller sent electric sparks through water, methane, ammonia, and hydrogen sealed in glass. Within a week the water had turned brown, and it held amino acids.

History Note

  • Friedrich Wöhler makes urea from inorganic starting materials (1828). Wikipedia: Wöhler synthesis
  • Erwin Schrödinger: an organism feeds on "negative entropy" (What Is Life?, 1944). Wikipedia: What Is Life?
  • James Watson and Francis Crick propose the double helix (1953). Wikipedia: Nucleic acid double helix
  • Hans Jonas, The Phenomenon of Life (1966); Terrence Deacon, Incomplete Nature (2011): how life and purpose emerge from thermodynamics. Wikipedia: Incomplete Nature

Scope Note

Metabolism is not only biochemistry: it presupposes molecularity and kinetics, and physiology and ecology presuppose it. The amoeba moves its atoms; the atoms don't move the amoeba. Metabolism names the pattern, not the norm: survival is the norm of metabolic practice. The nomenclature leaves open how life emerged.
