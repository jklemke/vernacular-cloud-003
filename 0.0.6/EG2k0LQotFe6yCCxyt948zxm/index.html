Definition

Situation is the grammatical operation of specifying something in time and space: before and after, above and below, here and now, at a place, at a time, for some time, for a long time. The paradigmatic situation is an actuality, established metaphysically and metalinguistically through the Unsayables: time, space, pattern, isolate, aggregate, void, and subjectivity. Further situations, fictional ones for example, can be established metaphorically.

Notes

Example

Once upon a time, in a land far, far away …

The storyteller situates a fiction with the same words we use to situate the actual: a time, a place, a distance.

History Note

  • Aristotle counts "where" (pou) and "when" (pote) among the ten categories (Cat. 4, 1b25–2a4).
  • Kant makes space and time the forms of all outer and inner intuition (KrV A22/B37–A49/B66).
  • Hans Reichenbach analyzes tense by three points: the time of speech, of the event, and of reference (Elements of Symbolic Logic, 1947, §51).
  • Jon Barwise and John Perry build a semantics on situations rather than possible worlds (Situations and Attitudes, 1983). SEP: Situations in Natural Language Semantics

Scope Note

A situation is the foundational thing, object, event, or entity: the thing as it is. Grammatically it is the verbiage that locates a nominate clause within its presuppositional world. Natural Semantic Metalanguage gives the primes it draws on: THIS, HERE, NOW, WHERE~PLACE, WHEN~TIME, BEFORE, AFTER, ABOVE, BELOW, FOR SOME TIME, A LONG TIME. In the Myth of the Nomen, "situation" is our elusive word par excellence.
