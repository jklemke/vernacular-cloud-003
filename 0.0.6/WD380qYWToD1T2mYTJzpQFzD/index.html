Definition

Cohesion is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of a single, isolated body, such as a planet with its rocks, oceans, atmosphere, and weather, or a star with its fusion and convection. The body's macrostate drives its parts: a star's mass and heat fuse its hydrogen into helium.

Example

The coastlines of South America and Africa fit together, and the same small freshwater reptile, Mesosaurus, lies fossilized in rocks on both sides of the South Atlantic.

History Note

  • James Hutton reads deep time in the rocks: "no vestige of a beginning, no prospect of an end" (Theory of the Earth, 1788). Wikipedia: James Hutton
  • Charles Lyell's Principles of Geology (1830–33). Wikipedia: Principles of Geology
  • Alfred Wegener proposes continental drift (1912, 1915); plate tectonics confirms it in the 1960s. Wikipedia: Plate tectonics
  • Hans Bethe explains how fusion powers the stars (1939). Wikipedia: Stellar nucleosynthesis

Scope Note

Cohesion is not a discipline: geology, meteorology, oceanography, and stellar astrophysics all work in it. A star belongs here as a cohesive body and to cosmology as a member of a distributed system; a framework, unlike a discipline, need not claim the whole object.
