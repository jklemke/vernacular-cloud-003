Definition

Isolate is the ineffable notion of the one: whatever is singled out as this, distinct from what surrounds it. We pick out isolates all the time, yet every attempt to say what makes something one thing appeals to boundaries, identity, or counting, and each of these already singles something out.

Like each of the Unsayables, isolate cannot be defined; we must explicate it by use, in the prototype sentence: Subjectivity detects patterns of isolates, aggregates, and voids in time and space.

Notes

Example

Point at a cloud and say "that one". The cloud has no sharp edge, and still the pointing succeeds.

History Note

  • Aristotle's primary substance (ousia) is the individual "this", such as this man or this horse (Cat. 5, 2a11–14).
  • Duns Scotus names the "thisness" of an individual haecceitas: see haecceity. Wikipedia: Haecceity
  • Leibniz holds that no two things are exactly alike: the identity of indiscernibles. Wikipedia: Identity of indiscernibles

Scope Note

Isolate and aggregate are poles, not kinds: what is one isolate under one description is an aggregate under another (a forest, a tree, a cell). The pair lets us speak of objecthood and generality without deciding between realism and nominalism.
