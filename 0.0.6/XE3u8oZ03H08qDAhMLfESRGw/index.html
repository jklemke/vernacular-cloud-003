Definition

Technē (τέχνη) is the Greek word for craft, art, and skill: a teachable way of making that knows what it is doing and why. The doctor, the shipwright, the flute-player, and the rhetorician each had a technē.

Socrates made technē his model of knowledge; Plato asked whether rhetoric was a technē or only a knack; Aristotle set it against nature (physis) and against action (praxis). Its descendants divide: technique keeps the skilled hand, while technology becomes the instrumental mastery Heidegger called enframing.

Example

τέχνην δὲ αὐτὴν οὔ φημι εἶναι ἀλλ' ἐμπειρίαν, ὅτι οὐκ ἔχει λόγον οὐδένα.

"I say it is not a craft but a knack, because it can give no account."

Socrates on cookery, Plato, Gorgias 465a

History Note

  • Socrates repeatedly takes the crafts as his model of knowledge: the doctor and the pilot know what they do and can teach it (Republic I, 341c–342e; Laches). In the Gorgias he denies that rhetoric and cookery are crafts at all, since they cannot give an account (logos) (462b–465a).
  • Aristotle: technē arises from experience when a universal judgment is formed (Metaphysics A.1, 981a5–12), and is a disposition to make things with a true account (Nicomachean Ethics VI.4, 1140a20–21). Things by nature have their principle of change in themselves, things by craft outside themselves (Physics II.1).
  • Latin renders it ars (the liberal arts, artes liberales); Arabic ṣināʿa. The adjective technikos gives "technical". Wikipedia: Techne
  • "Technology" spreads in the 19th century (Jacob Bigelow, Elements of Technology, 1829). Heidegger argues that modern technology is no longer technē but a way of revealing that "enframes" (Gestell) the world as a stock of resources ("The Question Concerning Technology", 1954).

Scope Note

Technē is not technology. The move from technē to the technical, from a skill embedded in a life to a procedure abstracted from it, is the move this nomenclature tracks in the jurisdiction of technology. What the Greeks meant by craft is closer to enaction guided by discernment: knowing how, not only knowing that.
