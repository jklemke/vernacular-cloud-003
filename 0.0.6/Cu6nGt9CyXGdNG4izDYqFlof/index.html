Definition

Oubliation is the monster of the labyrinth of reasons that makes us forget: to eliminate an essential concept from one's foundation vocabulary, or actively to ignore it, and then to explain away what has been forgotten. The word comes from the oubliette, the dungeon of forgetting.

The classic case is the elimination of consciousness: the third-person scientist must oubliate their own subjectivity. Oubliation is the opposite of discernment. It is one of the two monsters Badinage adds to the classical tropes.

Notes

Example

"Whoever blots out the notion of consciousness from his list of first principles must still provide in some way for that function's being carried on."

William James, "Does 'Consciousness' Exist?" (1904)

To blot out a first principle is, in Badinage's word, to oubliate it.

Theseus, who escaped the labyrinth, was an accomplished oubliator. He forgot Ariadne on Naxos, forgot to change his black sails for white, and in Hades sat down on the Chair of Forgetfulness and could not rise.

The word "knowledge" oubliates the distinction between sentient and sapient practice.

Scope Note

Not all oubliation is a fault. To select is to omit; to suspend is to oubliate. Granularity is combinatorially explosive, so we must oubliate what is not being actively discerned. Oubliation leads astray when the omission is forgotten, and the theory goes on to deny what it omitted.

History Note

  • Badinage coins the term from the French oublier, "to forget", by way of the oubliette, a dungeon entered only by a trapdoor in its ceiling.
  • William James denies that consciousness is an entity but insists that it names a function, which cannot be blotted out ("Does 'Consciousness' Exist?", 1904).
  • Richard Rorty's "disappearance" form of the identity theory (1965), and Paul and Patricia Churchland's eliminative materialism, propose that mental terms will drop out of a mature science. SEP: Eliminative Materialism
  • Daniel Dennett's "Quining Qualia" (1988) argues that qualia, as philosophers describe them, do not exist.
