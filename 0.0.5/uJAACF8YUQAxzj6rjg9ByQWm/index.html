Definition

Physiology is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of the organism: how cells, tissues, organs, and organ systems work individually and together to keep a living body alive, from a bacterium's flagellum to a human heart.

Example

William Harvey tied a cord around a man's arm, pressed along the swollen veins, and showed that the blood in them flows only towards the heart (De Motu Cordis, 1628).

History Note

  • William Harvey demonstrates the circulation of the blood (De Motu Cordis, 1628). Wikipedia: De Motu Cordis
  • Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann: all living things are made of cells (1838–39). Wikipedia: Cell theory
  • Claude Bernard's milieu intérieur: the organism keeps its internal environment constant (1865). Wikipedia: Milieu intérieur
  • Walter Cannon names it homeostasis (The Wisdom of the Body, 1932). Wikipedia: Homeostasis

Scope Note

Physiology is not one discipline: medicine, neuroscience, botany, and microbiology all work in it. It follows a body whose every part is replaced, a living Ship of Theseus, by its metabolic continuity. It describes how an organism works, not what it is like to be one: sentience belongs to the Practical Realms.
