Definition

Circularity is the monster of the labyrinth of reasons when the end of all our exploring is to arrive where we started. When the proof of a claim presupposes the claim itself. Logicians call it circulus in probando, the circle in proving, begging the question, or presuming the conclusion.

It is the logical circle of the Münchhausen trilemma and the fifth mode of Agrippa.

Broader

  • labyrinth of reasons

Notes

Alternative Label

  • assuming the consequent
  • begging the question

Example

Every word in a dictionary is defined by other words in the same dictionary. Follow the definitions far enough and you come back to the word you started from.

Scope Note

Not every circle is vicious. A dictionary is circular, and so are the Unsayables, an interdependent closure set in which each term becomes meaningful only through the others. A circle becomes vicious when it claims to prove.

History Note

  • Aristotle analyzes begging the question, in Latin petitio principii (An. Pr. II.16; Top. VIII.13). Wikipedia: Begging the question
  • Agrippa's fifth mode, the reciprocal (diallēlos): what should confirm the matter needs the matter to confirm it (Sextos Empeirikos, PH I.169).
  • Arnauld charges Descartes with the "Cartesian circle": clear and distinct perception proves that God exists, and God guarantees clear and distinct perception (Fourth Objections, AT VII 214). SEP: Descartes' Epistemology
  • Hans Albert's Münchhausen trilemma (1968) makes the circle one of the three ends of every attempt at ultimate justification. Wikipedia: Circular reasoning
  • Douglas Hofstadter finds the same shape, a "strange loop", in Gödel's self-referring sentences, Escher's drawings, and Bach's canons (Gödel, Escher, Bach, 1979).
