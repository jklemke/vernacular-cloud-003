Definition

A Neoplatonic realm is a word of Ancient Greek that marked out a region of thought for the Socratic-Abrahamic inheritance. The Neoplatonic Realms trace twenty-four such words from the pre-Socratics to the Neoplatonic synthesis of late antiquity, and from there into the science, literature, theology, and philosophy of the present day.

They serve two purposes. They are a second map of the nomenclature, older than the first, to compare and contrast with it; and they are a reference for tracing how each word's form and meaning changed. None of these words ever had a single, clearly definable meaning. To demand one is the Socratic Miscarriage.

Example

Κρατύλος φησὶν ὅδε, ὦ Σώκρατες, ὀνόματος ὀρθότητα εἶναι ἑκάστῳ τῶν ὄντων φύσει πεφυκυῖαν.

"Cratylus here says, Socrates, that there is a correctness of name for each thing that is, belonging to it by nature." Plato, Cratylus 383a

History Note

  • Plato's Cratylus asks whether names belong to things by nature (Cratylus) or by convention (Hermogenes), and plays at etymologies of Greek words for a whole dialogue without settling the question. It is the background of this section. Wikipedia: Cratylus (dialogue)
  • Plotinos (c. 204/5–270) gives Platonism its late form; his student Porphyry (c. 234–c. 305) edits the Enneads and writes the Isagoge, an introduction to Aristotle's Categories. Porphyry is a convenient marker of the synthesis. Wikipedia: Neoplatonism
  • Origen (c. 185–c. 253), Proklos (412–485), and Pseudo-Dionysios (c. 500) carry the same vocabulary into Christian theology and its hierarchies. Wikipedia: Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite
  • The Graeco-Arabic translation movement in Baghdad (8th–10th centuries) renders the Greek words into Arabic; the Latin translations of Toledo (12th century) bring many of them back into Europe through Arabic. Wikipedia: Graeco-Arabic translation movement

Scope Note

The Neoplatonic Realms are not conceptualization, which in this nomenclature names an act; they are a historical vocabulary. They are listed in the order of their counterparts in the rest of the nomenclature: archē, ontos, and ousia beside the Unsayables; doxa and epistēmē beside the Labyrinth of Reasons; telos, ēthos, and alētheia beside the Norms; mythos beside the Myths; gramma and logos beside the Grammar; eidos beside the Semantics; physis, dynamis, energeia, hylē, and morphē beside the Physical Realms; psychē, pneuma, praxis, technē, nous, sophia, and theōria beside the Practical Realms. Seldom does one Greek word match one Badinagean concept.
