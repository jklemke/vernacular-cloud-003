Definition

The Myth of the Given is the spell that falls on sentient practice when perception is taken to be the reception of facts: the notion that we perceive facts. The Given is not the recept; it is the output of a complex perceptual process.

The name is Sellars'; the diagnosis is turned around. Sellars was right that the given is a myth, but wrong to conclude that all knowledge must therefore be inferential.

Notes

Example

"I presume that no philosopher who has attacked the philosophical idea of givenness or, to use the Hegelian term, immediacy has intended to deny that there is a difference between inferring that something is the case and, for example, seeing it to be the case."

Sellars, EPM §1

What the subject sees are the red and bulgy aspects of a conjectured tomato-object.

History Note

  • Bertrand Russell distinguishes knowledge by acquaintance, of sense-data, from knowledge by description (The Problems of Philosophy, 1912).
  • H. H. Price: "When I see a tomato there is much that I can doubt", but not that there is a red, round, bulgy patch (Perception, 1932).
  • Wilfrid Sellars attacks "the entire framework of givenness" (Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind, 1956). SEP: Wilfrid Sellars
  • John McDowell holds that experience is conceptual all the way out, and recommends a Wittgensteinian quietism (Mind and World, 1994).

Scope Note

We must not confuse recognition with signification, or take sapient praxis as the model for sentient praxis. The perceptual is not an arbitrary signifier presented to an enduring subject, nor a deterministic effect of some cause "out there"; it is an immediate conjecture of similarity to past experience, a selection from received sensations to which subjectivity and objectivity have been attached. Sentient practice is non-inferential because it is non-verbal, but it is not without norms: it is corroboration. Sellars, insisting that knowledge must be inferential, falls into the Myth of the Proven. Sentient practice is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Given.
