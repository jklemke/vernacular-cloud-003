Definition

Ecology is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of living systems: populations, communities, ecosystems, and the biosphere, and the flows of energy and matter among organisms and their environment. Of the seven it is the most emergent and the least settled: its patterns arise from the interaction of many independent organisms.

History Note

  • Ernst Haeckel coins Ökologie, from Greek oikos, house (1866). Wikipedia: Ecology
  • Vladimir Vernadsky, The Biosphere (1926). Wikipedia: Biosphere
  • Arthur Tansley coins "ecosystem" (1935). Wikipedia: Ecosystem
  • Raymond Lindeman traces energy through the levels of a food web ("The Trophic-Dynamic Aspect of Ecology", 1942). Wikipedia: Trophic level

Scope Note

Ecology is not one discipline: population biology, evolutionary biology, and earth-system science all work in it. As cosmology interprets bodies among bodies, ecology interprets organisms among organisms. Its patterns are real, but its predictions are local and probabilistic: here the Physical Realms shade into history, and into the Practical Realms.
