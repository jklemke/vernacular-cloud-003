Definition

Absolute Perspective is the puzzle that reason meets when it asks, "Is there a view from everywhere?"

Thesis: there is a standpoint from which the whole world can be seen and every outcome calculated.

Antithesis: every perspective is local; there is no global perspective, though there is an abstract, generalized one.

It is Kant's third antinomy, of freedom and natural causality.

Notes

Example

If a tree falls in a forest and there is no one around, does it make a sound?

"Determinism is incompatible with fundamental physics. There is no Absolute Perspective from which to calculate outcomes."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Kant's third antinomy: besides causality by the laws of nature there is a causality through freedom; there is no freedom, and everything happens by the laws of nature (KrV A444/B472).
  • Nietzsche: "There is only a perspective seeing, only a perspective 'knowing'" (Genealogy of Morals III.12).
  • Einstein shows that simultaneity depends on the observer's frame of reference ("On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies", 1905).
  • Thomas Nagel examines the "view from nowhere", the objective standpoint we can approach but never fully occupy (The View from Nowhere, 1986). Wikipedia: Perspectivism

Scope Note

The tree in the forest is the antinomy in miniature. The recommendation is to label it as an antinomy rather than to argue about the details. Absolute Perspective is the counterpart of prejudice: since every perspective is local, reasoning always starts where someone stands.
