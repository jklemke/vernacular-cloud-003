Definition

The legitimator is the Name of God as the source of normativity: the authority behind every other authority, from the divine right of kings to the Ladder of Orthodoxy.

Is there a legitimator? No, but there is legitimacy, and it is grounded elsewhere: sentience is the ultimate legitimate jurisdiction. The deistic gambit runs the other way, from God to the LORD to whoever claims to speak for him.

History Note

  • Plato's Euthyphro: is the pious loved by the gods because it is pious, or pious because they love it? (10a).
  • Plato's noble lie, a myth told to secure the city's order (Republic III, 414b–415d).
  • James VI and I defends the divine right of kings (The True Law of Free Monarchies, 1598).
  • Max Weber: traditional, charismatic, and rational-legal authority (Economy and Society, 1922). Wikipedia: Tripartite classification of authority

Scope Note

The legitimator is the hinge of the deistic gambit: once God is the source of normativity, the Shepherd's Crook is close at hand. The question that remains is how human institutions can be legitimate without a LORD.
