Definition

Absolute Necessity is the puzzle that reason meets when it asks, "Must everything be as it is?"

Thesis: something exists necessarily, a necessary being or necessary truths on which everything contingent depends.

Antithesis: nothing is necessary; everything could have been otherwise.

It is Kant's fourth antinomy.

Notes

Example

Two plus two must make four. The sun need not rise tomorrow. Hume watched one billiard ball strike another and saw motion follow motion, but never the necessity that was supposed to bind them (Enquiry VII).

Necessity is a design criterion of mathematics.

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Aristotle distinguishes the senses of "necessary": what cannot be otherwise, what is compulsory, what is required for a good (Metaph. Δ.5).
  • Avicenna divides existence into the necessary and the possible, and argues that the possible depends at last on a being necessary in itself (wājib al-wujūd). SEP: Ibn Sina's Metaphysics
  • Leibniz separates truths of reason, whose opposite is impossible, from truths of fact, whose opposite is possible (Monadology §33).
  • Kant's fourth antinomy: there belongs to the world a necessary being; no necessary being exists, in the world or outside it (KrV A452/B480).
  • Saul Kripke argues that some truths are necessary yet known only by experience: water is H₂O (Naming and Necessity, 1980).

Scope Note

Necessity belongs to the languages we engineer, logic and mathematics, where it is built in by design. The Myth of the Proven takes a design criterion for a feature of the world. The Lord's Leap goes one step further and turns the necessary being of the philosophers into a political authority.
