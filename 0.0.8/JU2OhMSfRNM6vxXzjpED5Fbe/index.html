Definition

Logos (λόγος) is the Greek word for what is said and for the reckoning behind it: word, speech, story, account, argument, reason, ratio, proportion, definition. It comes from legein, to gather, to count, to say.

Herakleitos made it the order of the cosmos; the sophists, the power of persuasion; Plato, the account that turns true opinion into knowledge; Aristotle, rational speech and the definition; the Stoics, the reason that runs through all things; John, the Word that was God. No single meaning runs through them all.

History Note

  • Herakleitos: "listening not to me but to the logos, it is wise to agree that all things are one" (fr. B50); most people never grasp it (B1).
  • Gorgias: "logos is a mighty lord" (λόγος δυνάστης μέγας ἐστίν), able to stop fear and kindle joy (Encomium of Helen 8).
  • Plato's Theaetetus tries knowledge as true opinion with a logos and finds three meanings of logos, none of which will do (201c–210a). Aristotle: of all animals only the human being has logos (Politics I.2, 1253a9–10).
  • The Stoics' logos spermatikos, the seminal reason in things; Philo of Alexandria's Logos, the intermediary between God and the world; John's prologue; Justin Martyr and Origen join them. Wikipedia: Logos
  • Latin splits the word: ratio (reason, reckoning) and verbum (word); Erasmus's Latin New Testament of 1519 renders John 1:1 In principio erat sermo, and is attacked for it. Arabic splits it three ways: kalām (speech, and so theology, ʿilm al-kalām), nuṭq (rational speech; the human being is ḥayawān nāṭiq), and manṭiq (logic).
  • Goethe's Faust tries "Im Anfang war das Wort", then Sinn, then Kraft, and settles on Tat, the deed (Faust I, 1224–1237). Derrida names the privilege of speech and presence "logocentrism" (1967). Modern descendants: logic, -logy, analogy, logo.

Scope Note

Logos is the clearest case against the Socratic Miscarriage: one word with a dozen incompatible senses, each of them used with full confidence, and the Theaetetus itself fails to fix it. In this nomenclature what logos named is spread across sapience, the magisterium of LANGUAGE, discourse, and the Myth of the Proven, which is the belief that logos alone can do the work of mythos.
