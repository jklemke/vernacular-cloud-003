Definition

Distance, duration, and energy are a conceptual device of WORLD: how far, how long, and how much. Distance measures space, duration measures time, and energy measures what changes within them; none of the three can be measured except against the other two.

They are the Unsayables space and time made measurable, and the quantity physics describes. Emmy Noether showed why the three belong together: energy is conserved because the laws of physics do not change with time, as momentum is conserved because they do not change with place.

Example

The metre is the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.

History Note

  • Aristotle's categories include where and when, acting and being acted upon (Categories 4, 1b25–2a4).
  • Newton's absolute space and absolute time (Principia, Scholium to the Definitions, 1687).
  • Einstein joins space and time, and matter and energy: E = mc² (1905). Wikipedia: Mass–energy equivalence
  • Emmy Noether proves that every continuous symmetry of a physical system has a conserved quantity (1918). Wikipedia: Noether's theorem
  • Since 1983 the metre has been defined by the speed of light and the second. Wikipedia: Metre

Scope Note

These are not the Unsayables themselves, which are preconditions and resist definition, but their measures. Nor are they kinetics, the interpretive framework that uses them.
