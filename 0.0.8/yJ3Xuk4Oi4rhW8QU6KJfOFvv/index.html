Definition

A word is the most basic unit of signification: it refers to a recognizable object or event. The word already carries all the metaphysical weight of names, existent being, imaginary being, abstract entities, the indefinite (a dog) and the definite (the dog), qualities and tropes (red), and actions (walk, breathe).

Linguists still have difficulty describing what a word is; word, morpheme, and lexeme do not line up neatly, and no one definition holds across all languages.

Example

Fido, a dog, the dog, dog, unicorn, seven, red, walk, breathe: each is a word, and each carries a different metaphysics.

History Note

  • Aristotle: spoken words are symbols of affections in the soul, and written words are symbols of spoken words (De Int. 1, 16a3–8).
  • Jan Baudouin de Courtenay introduces the morpheme as the smallest meaningful part of a word (1880s). Wikipedia: Morpheme
  • Leonard Bloomfield defines the word as a "minimum free form" (Language, 1933). Wikipedia: Word
  • Martin Haspelmath argues that there is no cross-linguistically valid definition of the word ("The indeterminacy of word segmentation and the nature of morphology and syntax", Folia Linguistica, 2011). Wikipedia: Lexeme

Scope Note

A word is a semantic unit that forms part of a sentence. It is where the token illusion begins: a token is identical to itself, and the word lends that identity to whatever it names.
