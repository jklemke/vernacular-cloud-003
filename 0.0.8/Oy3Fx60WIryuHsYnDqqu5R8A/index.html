Definition

Ideology is the jurisdiction loose enough to hold religion, philosophy, politics, and scientism: any institutionalized picture of how things are and how they ought to be, held in common and defended. At this level of abstraction science, religion, philosophy, and ritual all sit here.

That the word has a negative connotation is part of its charm: you have an ideology; I have common sense. Its tribunal is the court of public opinion.

History Note

  • Destutt de Tracy coins idéologie, a science of ideas (1796). Wikipedia: Ideology
  • Marx and Engels: ideology inverts reality, as in a camera obscura (The German Ideology, 1846).
  • Karl Mannheim: ideology and utopia, the thought of those who defend an order and of those who would overturn it (Ideologie und Utopie, 1929).
  • Louis Althusser: church, school, and family as ideological state apparatuses ("Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses", 1970).
  • Terry Eagleton surveys the meanings of the word (Ideology: An Introduction, 1991).

Scope Note

Ideology here is not only false consciousness: it is the neutral name for every institutionalized worldview, the speaker's own included. It is not education, though the two contend over what counts as knowledge.
