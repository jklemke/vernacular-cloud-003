Definition

The Myth of the Proven is the spell that falls on logical practice when deduction is misapplied to the whole of reality: the myth of necessary truth and determinate outcomes. It starts with the notion of deductive calculation and extrapolates to a physical notion of necessity, as if the universe were an intellect.

It is closely related to the doctrine of determinism and to the antinomy of Absolute Necessity. It gives The Myth of the Proven its title.

Notes

Example

"Philosophy is written in this grand book, the universe … It is written in the language of mathematics."

Galileo, The Assayer (1623)

The very idea that the sentence "snow is white" points to a fact that snow is white--this is the Myth of the Proven

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • The Pythagoreans hold that the principles of mathematics are the principles of all things (Aristotle, Metaph. A.5, 985b23–26).
  • Spinoza sets out his Ethics "in geometrical order", and Leibniz makes the principle of sufficient reason a law of everything that is (Monadology §32).
  • Laplace's intellect, knowing every force and position, would see the future as present: determinism as the Myth of the Proven applied to physics (Philosophical Essay on Probabilities, 1814).
  • Eugene Wigner wonders at "the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in the natural sciences" (1960).
  • Max Tegmark proposes that the physical world is a mathematical structure (Our Mathematical Universe, 2014). Wikipedia: Mathematical universe hypothesis

Editorial Note

Note elaborate examples from history of attempts to create a Euclidian-flavoured, axiomatic philosophy of determinism: Hobbes, Spinoza, Hegel, Wittgenstein

Scope Note

The Myth of the Proven is the notion that facts are foundational. It is the oubliation of sentience by sapience, and the clever ruse of the analytic philosopher: to posit a world in which I don't exist. It confuses token identity with object equivalence, and so rests on the token illusion. Logical practice is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Proven; the spell is broken, the practice stands.
