Definition

The Names of God are seven notions, the creator, controller, provider, comforter, saviour, legitimator, and magistrator, that overlap significantly with what people mean by "God", "gods", or "a higher power". Does God exist? No, but we are left with this cluster of abstract, metaphorical notions, each of which the nomenclature has met before under another name.

In the Spinozistic sense, God is that which combines world, norm, and concept into a single, all-encompassing subjectivity: the mind-dependent notions of a mind-independent world (MDNMIW), norm (MDNMIN), and concept (MDNMIC), gathered into one. Badinage acknowledges this God, appreciates it, and in the end deprecates it.

The Socratic-Abrahamic inheritance brought us to the question through the Socratic Miscarriage, the demand for absolutes and definitions, and the Lord's Leap, a logical conundrum resolved by positing a universal sovereign. The question is not whether there is a God, but whether there is a LORD God: can we have God without LORD?

History Note

  • God answers Moses: "I am who I am" (Exodus 3:14, NRSVue).
  • The ninety-nine most beautiful names of God in Islam (al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā; Qurʾan 7:180). Wikipedia: Names of God in Islam
  • Pseudo-Dionysios the Areopagite, On the Divine Names (c. 500). SEP: Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite
  • Maimonides: God can be described only by negations (Guide for the Perplexed I.58, c. 1190).
  • Spinoza: Deus sive Natura, God or Nature (Ethics IV, Preface, 1677). SEP: Baruch Spinoza
  • Ludwig Feuerbach: God is the projected essence of humanity (The Essence of Christianity, 1841); Émile Durkheim: the god of the clan is the clan itself, transfigured (The Elementary Forms of Religious Life, 1912).

Scope Note

The Names of God close the nomenclature, and they are the conclusion the Systematic Thesis has been moving toward. They are not religion, which sits among the Jurisdictions under ideology. The breath, the speech, and the wisdom of God (pneuma, logos, sophia) are among the Neoplatonic Realms. The question of God, put another way, is whether there is a vantage point in the apeiron.
