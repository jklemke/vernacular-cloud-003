Definition

A myth is a narrative, conceptual, transcendent explanation that, while not necessarily true in the sense of corresponding to actuality, bears a pragmatic, edificational message. The Myths are the varieties of metaphysics. Each casts a spell over a practice, and to fall under that spell is to occult the practice: to take a thing, a practice, or an event in the real world and assign it supernatural properties (occultation).

Badinage counts seven: the Myths of the Proven, the Given, the Coven, the Nomen, the Scriven, the Maven, and the Haven. The myths will not help us solve the antinomies; they may, however, help us cope with them.

Example

A founding document becomes holy writ; an explorer becomes a saint, then a villain; a revolutionary's portrait hangs over the gate of the capital. The US Constitution, Christopher Columbus, and Mao have each been occulted.

"Sentient praxis is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Given. Sapient praxis is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Proven. Social praxis is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Coven."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Plato sets mythos beside logos and ends dialogues with myths of judgment and the soul, such as the myth of Er (Rep. X, 614b–621d). SEP: Plato's Myths
  • Durkheim defines the sacred as things set apart and forbidden, and religion as the beliefs and practices that unite a community around them (The Elementary Forms of Religious Life, 1912).
  • Wilfrid Sellars names the "Myth of the Given" (EPM, 1956); Badinage borrows the form for all seven.
  • Roland Barthes reads modern myth as speech that turns history into nature (Mythologies, 1957).
  • Hans Blumenberg treats myth as work that makes bearable the "absolutism of reality" (Work on Myth, 1979).

Scope Note

Each myth answers a question:

  • Can we participate in logic without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Proven?
  • Can we participate in sentience without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Given?
  • Can we participate in society without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Coven?
  • Can we participate in ostension without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Nomen?
  • Can we participate in culture without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Scriven?
  • Can we participate in legitimacy without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Maven?
  • Can we participate in edification without falling under the spell of the Myth of the Haven?

Perhaps all the Myths can be seen as attempts to retrieve the oubliated apeiron and the oubliated ipseity. And Badinage is offering a myth too: the strange loop of the Badinagean myth.
