Definition

WORLD is the contested space of questions like "What is out there, and what happens in it?" It is the mind-independent world as a mind-dependent notion: the contradiction at the heart of MDNMIW, accepted as a precondition of sentience rather than solved. Its conceptual devices are distance-duration-energy and event-system-scenario.

What Kant was after with the pure forms of intuition is better called a precondition: that which is detected in the prototype sentence of the Unsayables, Subjectivity detects patterns of isolates, aggregates, and voids in time and space. Accepting the contradiction does not settle materialism against idealism, but it reduces the puzzle to its one fundamental contradiction.

History Note

  • Kant: space and time are pure forms of sensible intuition, not properties of things in themselves (KrV A19–49/B33–73).
  • Kant calls it a scandal of philosophy that the existence of things outside us must be taken on faith (KrV Bxxxix note).
  • Wittgenstein: "The world is all that is the case" (Tractatus 1, 1921).
  • Wilfrid Sellars sets the manifest image of the world beside the scientific image ("Philosophy and the Scientific Image of Man", 1962). SEP: Wilfrid Sellars

Scope Note

WORLD is not the cosmos of cosmology, nor the totality of ENTIRETY: it is the world as it presents itself to a sentient being, local and bounded. The Physical Realms are its interpretive frameworks. The antinomy at home in WORLD is Absolute Beginning.
