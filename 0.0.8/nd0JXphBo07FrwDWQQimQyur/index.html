Definition

IPSEITY is the contested space of questions like "Who perceives?" It is an attempt to articulate what it is to be engaged in sentient, social, and sapient practice: the embodied, situated, first-person locus of perception, action, and discourse. Its conceptual devices are recept-aspect-object and external-internal-central.

Subjectivity is the truly ineffable notion of being a subject in the world; IPSEITY is a candidate articulation of it, alongside Heidegger's Dasein, the Cartesian ego, the immaterial soul, and the self and no-self of the Hindu and Buddhist traditions. Ego is meant literally (Latin, "I"), not in Freud's sense.

History Note

  • Descartes: "I think, therefore I am" (Discourse on the Method IV, 1637; Meditations II, 1641). Wikipedia: Cogito, ergo sum
  • Kant: the "I think" must be able to accompany all my representations (KrV B131–132).
  • The Upanishadic ātman and the Buddhist anattā: self and no-self. Wikipedia: Anattā
  • Heidegger names Dasein the being for whom its own being is an issue (Being and Time §4, 1927). Wikipedia: Dasein
  • David Chalmers distinguishes the hard problem of consciousness from the easy ones ("Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness", 1995). Wikipedia: Hard problem of consciousness

Scope Note

IPSEITY is not subjectivity, which stays unsayable; it is not the psychoanalytic ego, nor a proof of an immaterial soul. Solipsism and the hard problem of consciousness are puzzles asked here. The antinomy at home in IPSEITY is Absolute Perspective.
