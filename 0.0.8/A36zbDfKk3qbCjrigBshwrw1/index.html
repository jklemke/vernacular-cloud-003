Definition

Compatibility, incompatibility, and consequence are a conceptual device of CONTINGENCY: compatibility is what can stand together; incompatibility is what excludes; consequence is what follows. Each is understood through the others. In Robert Brandom's incompatibility semantics, a claim follows from another when whatever is incompatible with the conclusion is also incompatible with the premise.

These are relations among actualizations, not causes: a consequence is not an effect. In the world they constrain what can happen together; in discourse, what can be said together.

Example

A ball cannot be red all over and green all over at once; if it is scarlet, it follows that it is red.

History Note

  • Aristotle: the same attribute cannot at the same time belong and not belong to the same subject in the same respect (Metaphysics Γ.3, 1005b19–20).
  • Leibniz: only compossible things can exist together in one world. Wikipedia: Compossibility
  • Kant's categories of Relation pair the hypothetical judgment with ground and consequence, and the disjunctive with community (KrV A80/B106).
  • Robert Brandom builds a semantics on incompatibility (Between Saying and Doing, 2008, ch. 5). Wikipedia: Robert Brandom

Scope Note

The device echoes Kant's Relation, without his causality: CONTINGENCY deprecates "cause". It is the worldly side of implication in the Grammar.
