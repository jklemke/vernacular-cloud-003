Definition

All, none, and final are a conceptual device of ENTIRETY: all is everything within a specified boundary, the whole; none is the empty whole, nothing within it; final is the last word, the claim that the boundary will not move.

All and none range over a domain that must be given in advance. The final is the boldest of the three: it closes the domain for good.

History Note

  • Aristotle's square of opposition sets "every" against "no" (De Interpretatione 7). SEP: The Traditional Square of Opposition
  • Frege introduces the universal quantifier (Begriffsschrift, 1879).
  • Russell's paradox: there is no set of all sets that do not contain themselves (1901; The Principles of Mathematics, 1903). SEP: Russell's Paradox
  • Eschatology, the doctrine of the last things. Wikipedia: Eschatology

Scope Note

The device echoes Kant's Quantity (unity, plurality, totality), loosely. "All" without a boundary is the Absolute, and the Absolute cannot be reached by the analysis of isolates and aggregates.
