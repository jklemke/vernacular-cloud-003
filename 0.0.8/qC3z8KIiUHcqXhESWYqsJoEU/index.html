Definition

The creator is the Name of God as the maker of the beginning: the one who brings the world out of nothing, or out of the formless deep.

Is there a creator? No, but there are preconditions: the Unsayables, time, space, and pattern, which nothing makes. The questions of origin remain, and the nomenclature asks them under Absolute Beginning, ENTIRETY, and the Greek archē. The Lord's Leap starts here: because there are preconditions, there must be a God, and because there is a God, there must be a Lord.

History Note

  • Plato's demiurge, a craftsman who shapes the cosmos after an eternal model (Timaeus 28a–29a).
  • Creation out of nothing, creatio ex nihilo (2 Maccabees 7:28; Irenaeus, Against Heresies II.10). Wikipedia: Creatio ex nihilo
  • Aquinas: that the world began cannot be proved; creation is dependence, not a first moment (Summa Theologiae I q.46 a.2).
  • Georges Lemaître's "primeval atom" (1931), the beginning of the big bang theory. Wikipedia: Georges Lemaître

Scope Note

The creator answers a question the nomenclature declines to ask from outside the boundary of ENTIRETY.
