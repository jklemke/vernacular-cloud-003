Definition

Meaning is the norm of communicative practice: what is said ought to be understood as it was meant. Meaning starts with ostension, among those who share sentient and social practice, and must move to description for those who do not; description opens the way to error, misinterpretation, subterfuge, and elaboration, and so to correction.

Notes

Example

A parent points at the dog and says "dog". The child, sharing the same scene, gets it. Later the child reads "dog" in a book about wolves and must work out what was meant.

History Note

  • Frege distinguishes the sense (Sinn) of an expression from its reference (Bedeutung) ("Über Sinn und Bedeutung", 1892). SEP: Gottlob Frege
  • Wittgenstein: for a large class of cases, the meaning of a word is its use in the language (PI §43).
  • Grice analyzes speaker meaning as an intention to produce an effect through the hearer's recognition of that intention ("Meaning", 1957).
  • Kripke's skeptic asks what fact makes it the case that I meant plus and not "quus" (Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language, 1982). Wikipedia: Kripkenstein

Scope Note

Wittgenstein says, "don't look to the meaning, look to the use." But the purpose of the meaning is to establish the use. Kripke's skeptic looks for a fact that fixes what a word means and finds none; on this account meaning is not a fact to be found but a norm of a shared practice, anchored in ostension.
