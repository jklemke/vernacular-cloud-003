Definition

Survival is the norm of metabolic practice: the first norm, and the one from which all the others emerge. With life, behaviour becomes viable or non-viable for the organism's own continued existence, and good and bad enter the world.

Broader

  • norm

Related

  • metabolism
  • teleology

Notes

Example

A bacterium swims up a sugar gradient and away from an acid. Nothing in the stone beside it is better or worse for the stone.

"Survival is teleology. The advent of life is the advent of purpose."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Spinoza: each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being, its conatus (Ethics III, P6).
  • Herbert Spencer coins "survival of the fittest" (1864); Darwin adopts it in the fifth edition of the Origin (1869). Wikipedia: Survival of the fittest
  • Hans Jonas finds in metabolism the first freedom and the first concern: the organism's being is its own doing, always at stake (The Phenomenon of Life, 1966).
  • Maturana and Varela define the living by autopoiesis, a network that produces and maintains itself (Autopoiesis and Cognition, 1980). Wikipedia: Autopoiesis

Scope Note

Non-living nature has structure and function, even causal powers at the level of the system (a star, a storm, a whirlpool), but no norms. Survival is where normativity begins. The nomenclature leaves open how life emerged; it holds only that norms emerged with it.
