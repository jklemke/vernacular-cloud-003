Definition

Name, description, and explanation are a conceptual device of LANGUAGE: progressively more general, verbalized, third-person models. A name picks something out; a description says what it is like; an explanation says why it is so, by placing it in a pattern.

Theory is a further level still: a regimented, corroborated, normatively suspended set of names, descriptions, and explanations.

History Note

  • J. S. Mill: proper names denote without connoting (A System of Logic I.ii.5, 1843).
  • Russell's theory of descriptions ("On Denoting", 1905). SEP: Descriptions
  • Hempel and Oppenheim's covering-law model of explanation ("Studies in the Logic of Explanation", 1948). SEP: Scientific Explanation
  • Saul Kripke: names are rigid designators (Naming and Necessity, 1980).

Scope Note

The three are not the operations of the Grammar (indication, predication) but the models those operations build. The content of a name is distinct from its social function; the spell that falls on naming is the Myth of the Nomen.
