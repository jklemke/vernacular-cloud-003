Definition

External, internal, and central are a conceptual device of IPSEITY: three domains to which the recept-aspect-object structure is applied. The external is the world beyond the body; the internal is the body itself; the central is the arena in which thought, memory, imagination, and conjecture come and go.

Think of a bear, a toothache, and a memory. You can run from the bear, but you can't run from the toothache or the memory. A toothache has a physical locus in the body; a memory is located in the same central realm in which the subject, conjectures, thoughts, and emotions come and go.

History Note

  • Descartes divides the thinking thing from the extended thing (Meditations VI, 1641). Wikipedia: Mind–body dualism
  • Kant distinguishes outer sense, whose form is space, from inner sense, whose form is time (KrV A22–23/B37).
  • Charles Sherrington names exteroception, interoception, and proprioception (The Integrative Action of the Nervous System, 1906). Wikipedia: Interoception

Scope Note

The three are not Descartes's two substances plus one: they are domains of a single IPSEITY. The cogito demands all three, but subjectivity is the precondition of the cogito and does not "exist" among them.
