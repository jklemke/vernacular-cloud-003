Definition

Ontos (ὄντος, "of being") is a form of the Greek verb "to be", and the stem from which the modern word ontology was coined. The Greeks spoke of to on (τὸ ὄν), "what is", and ta onta, "the things that are".

Parmenides made "it is" the whole of philosophy; Plato and Aristotle spent their lives answering him. Aristotle's answer is that being is said in many ways, which is to say that the word never had one meaning.

Example

Τὸ δὲ ὂν λέγεται μὲν πολλαχῶς.

"Being is said in many ways."

Aristotle, Metaphysics Γ.2, 1003a33

History Note

  • Parmenides sets the way of "it is" against the way of "it is not", and denies that what is not can be thought or said (frs. B2, B6). Wikipedia: Parmenides
  • Plato stages a "battle of gods and giants" over what being is, the friends of the Forms against the materialists, and makes not-being a kind of difference (Sophist 246a–c, 257b).
  • Aristotle: being is said in many ways, but always with reference to one, ousia (Metaphysics Γ.2, 1003a33–b10); its kinds are the categories.
  • Latin renders it ens and esse; Arabic wujūd (existence) and mawjūd (an existent). Ibn Sina distinguishes a thing's existence from its essence (māhiyya), a distinction the Latin schoolmen inherit.
  • Ontologia is coined in the early 17th century (Jacob Lorhard, 1606; Rudolf Goclenius, 1613) and made a discipline by Christian Wolff (1730). Heidegger revives the question of being (Being and Time, 1927). Wikipedia: Ontology

Scope Note

Ontos is not a definition of being, and this nomenclature offers none. What is real is held in place by corroboration under the norm of reality; the mind-independent world is the working assumption of sentience (MDNMIW). Ontology as a demand for one meaning of "to be" is the Socratic Miscarriage at its widest.
