Definition

Selectability, divisibility, and composability are a conceptual device of INFINITY: selectability asks whether one can be picked out; divisibility, whether it can be cut; composability, whether the parts can be put back together. The infinite strains all three at once.

Example

A solid ball can be cut into finitely many pieces and put back together as two balls, each the size of the first. (The Banach–Tarski paradox, 1924)

History Note

  • Zeno's paradoxes of division: to cross a distance one must first cross half of it, and so without end (Aristotle, Physics VI.9).
  • Democritus's atoms, the uncuttable. SEP: Ancient Atomism
  • Ernst Zermelo states the axiom of choice (1904). Wikipedia: Axiom of choice
  • Stefan Banach and Alfred Tarski show that, with the axiom of choice, a ball can be decomposed and recomposed as two (1924). Wikipedia: Banach–Tarski paradox

Scope Note

The antinomy at home here is Absolute Granularity. Molecularity is the physical framework of parts and wholes; this device is its mathematical shadow.
