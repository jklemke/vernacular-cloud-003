Definition

Technology is the jurisdiction of tools: means whose efficacy is measured by how well they manipulate or intervene in the Physical Realms, from shovels and baskets to turbines and satellites.

Its tribunal is the test, does it work?, and engineers, markets, and regulators render the verdict. Technology training is not a liberal education; here the two cultures part.

History Note

  • Francis Bacon: nature, to be commanded, must be obeyed (Novum Organum I.3, 1620).
  • Martin Heidegger: modern technology enframes the world as standing-reserve ("The Question Concerning Technology", 1954). SEP: Martin Heidegger
  • Ian Hacking: science is intervening as well as representing (Representing and Intervening, 1983).
  • C. P. Snow's two cultures (1959).

Scope Note

Technology is not science, which sits under ideology at this level of abstraction, nor technē, the Greek craft knowledge that is its forerunner among the Neoplatonic Realms. Its measure is efficacy in the Physical Realms, not truth.
