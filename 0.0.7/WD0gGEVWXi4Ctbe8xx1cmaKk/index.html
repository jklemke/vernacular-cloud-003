Definition

Space is the ineffable notion of here, there, and between. We locate things in it, measure across it, and move through it, yet every attempt to say what it is turns it into a container, a relation among things, or a coordinate system, each of which already assumes it.

Like each of the Unsayables, space cannot be defined; we must explicate it by use, in the prototype sentence: Subjectivity detects patterns of isolates, aggregates, and voids in time and space.

Notes

Example

The word "here" points from wherever it is said; no map can show it without a "you are here".

History Note

  • Plato's chōra, the receptacle of the Timaeus, is grasped only "by a kind of bastard reasoning" (Tim. 52b).
  • Aristotle defines place (topos) as the innermost boundary of the containing body (Phys. IV.4, 212a20).
  • Newton's absolute space and Leibniz's relational space met in the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence (1715–16).
  • Kant: we can think space empty of objects, but cannot represent that there is no space (KrV A24/B38–39). SEP: Kant's Views on Space and Time

Scope Note

The nomenclature takes no side between space as a container (Newton) and space as relations among things (Leibniz). It names what both presuppose.
