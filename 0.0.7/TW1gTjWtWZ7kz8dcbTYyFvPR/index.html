Definition

Cosmology is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of a distributed system: bodies among bodies, in star systems, galaxies, clusters, and the universe as a whole, held together by gravity and read by their light. Its patterns are the largest that science has corroborated, and they belong to systems, not to atoms: no planet's orbit can be calculated without a centre of gravity.

Example

In 1965 Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson could not rid their horn antenna in Holmdel, New Jersey, of a faint microwave hiss. It came from every direction, day and night: the afterglow of the early universe.

History Note

  • Nicolaus Copernicus puts the sun at the centre (De revolutionibus, 1543). Wikipedia: Copernican heliocentrism
  • Johannes Kepler's laws of planetary motion (1609, 1619). Wikipedia: Kepler's laws of planetary motion
  • Edwin Hubble finds that galaxies recede in proportion to their distance (1929). Wikipedia: Hubble's law
  • Penzias and Wilson detect the cosmic microwave background (1965). Wikipedia: Cosmic microwave background

Scope Note

Cosmology is not only astronomy. It presupposes kinetics and molecularity (a star's spectrum is chemistry), and its pattern of bodies among bodies reappears, living, in ecology. What happens within a single cohesive body belongs to cohesion. Absolute Beginning is a puzzle that reason meets at the edge of cosmology, not one of its results.
