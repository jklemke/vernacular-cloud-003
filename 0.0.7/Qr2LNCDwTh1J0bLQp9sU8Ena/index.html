Definition

Semantics is the study of signification at the levels of granularity at which meaning is assigned, interpreted, and critiqued: the word, the sentence, the discourse, the concept, the referent, ostension, and metaphor. Meaning is an extremely complex pattern that spans psychology, language, culture, reality, and imagination. We are not in a meaning crisis, we are in a meaning surfeit, a meaning tsunami.

Syntax engineers strings; grammar operates on them; semantics signifies.

History Note

  • Michel Bréal names the science of meaning la sémantique (1883) and develops it in Essai de sémantique (1897). Wikipedia: Semantics
  • C. K. Ogden and I. A. Richards draw the triangle of symbol, thought, and referent (The Meaning of Meaning, 1923). Wikipedia: Triangle of reference
  • Alfred Tarski gives a semantic definition of truth for formalized languages ("The Concept of Truth in Formalized Languages", 1933). Wikipedia: Semantic theory of truth
  • Richard Montague treats a natural language as a formal language with a model-theoretic semantics ("English as a Formal Language", 1970). Wikipedia: Montague grammar

Editorial Note

What are we trying to accomplish here? parts of speech? we might say word, clause, sentence, paragraph, treatise. Are we confusing syntax and semantics?

Scope Note

Semantics is not syntax and not grammar. Formal semantics, in the manner of Tarski and Montague, is a meaning-preserving computation, but one must start with a meaning. The seven are vantage points from which meaning is assigned, interpreted, and critiqued, not a theory of linguistics, and the list is still unformed.
