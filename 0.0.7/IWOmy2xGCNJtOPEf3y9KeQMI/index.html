Definition

A referent is the unit of signification that a word or sentence is about: its content, on the WORLD side of the MDNMIW, the concept side of MDNMIC, the norm side of MDNMIN. The referent is an asymmetric link from the mind to the mind-independent entity, and therefore straddles psychology, culture, reality, and imagination.

Example

"The truth-makers are the things-as-they-are; they are significant; they can be signified. But the signifier always leaves a not-yet-signified remainder of the thing-as-it-is."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Gottlob Frege distinguishes the reference (Bedeutung) of a sign from its sense (Sinn) ("Über Sinn und Bedeutung", 1892). Wikipedia: Sense and reference
  • C. K. Ogden and I. A. Richards put the referent at one corner of the triangle of symbol, thought, and referent (The Meaning of Meaning, 1923). Wikipedia: Triangle of reference
  • J. L. Austin and P. F. Strawson debate truth (symposium "Truth", 1950); Strawson objects that Austin puts sentence-shaped facts into the world.
  • Saul Kripke: a name refers rigidly, through a causal chain rather than a description (Naming and Necessity, 1980). Wikipedia: Causal theory of reference
  • Kit Fine pairs each sentence with the states that make it true ("Truthmaker Semantics", 2017).

Scope Note

To treat the referent as grammar-shaped is grammomorphic: it confuses the map for the territory. The token illusion, the Myth of the Nomen, nominalism, and descriptivism are all grammomorphic.
