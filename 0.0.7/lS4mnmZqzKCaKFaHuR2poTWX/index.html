Definition

The controller is the Name of God as the agent behind every event: not a sparrow falls without the Father; fire does not burn the cotton, God does.

Is there a controller? No, but there are constraints: systems that bound what can happen, and an actualization that is spontaneous, indexical, and local. The questions live in CONTINGENCY and Absolute Necessity. Determinism, which Badinage calls the last refuge of the LORD God, keeps the controller's seat warm with no one in it.

History Note

  • al-Ghazali: fire does not burn the cotton; God creates the burning (The Incoherence of the Philosophers, Seventeenth Discussion, 1095). SEP: Al-Ghazali
  • Nicolas Malebranche's occasionalism: God is the only true cause (The Search after Truth, 1674–75).
  • John Calvin on providence: nothing happens by chance (Institutes I.16, 1559).
  • Laplace's demon: an intellect that knew every force and position would see the future like the past (Essai philosophique sur les probabilités, 1814).

Scope Note

The controller is not the provider: control is agency, provision is sustenance.
