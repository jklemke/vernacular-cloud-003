Definition

EPIPHANY is the contested space of questions like "What are we here for?" It is the movement to a transcendent, generalized perspective on reality: eudaimonia, salvation, liberation, awakening, enlightenment, bodhi, moksha, nirvana. Its conceptual devices are percept-notion-concept and suspension-ostension-transcendence.

The Socratic-Abrahamic inheritance poses it as a choice: to live a full human life, as the Epicureans taught, or to escape a fallen world, as the Gnostics, the Neoplatonists, and much of Christianity taught. EPIPHANY is also the move up the semantic ladder from ostensive to metaphorical language. Are the Myths a solution to the Antinomies?

History Note

  • Aristotle: eudaimonia is an activity of soul in accordance with virtue (Nicomachean Ethics I.7, 1098a16).
  • Epicurus: the end of life is health of body and tranquillity of soul (Letter to Menoeceus 128).
  • Plotinos ends the Enneads with the flight of the alone to the alone (VI.9.11).
  • Paul: "Do not be conformed to this age, but be transformed by the renewing of the mind" (Romans 12:2, NRSVue).
  • Bodhi, awakening, and nirvāṇa in the Buddhist traditions; mokṣa, liberation, in the Hindu. Wikipedia: Moksha

Scope Note

EPIPHANY is not a religion, and not its institutions: those belong to the Jurisdictions and the Names of God. The spell that falls on its practice is the Myth of the Haven. The koan suggests the choice may be false: the awakened life looks like the ordinary one. No antinomy is at home here.
