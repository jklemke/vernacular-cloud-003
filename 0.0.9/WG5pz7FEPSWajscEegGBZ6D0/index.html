Definition

SOCIETY is the contested space of questions like "Who are we to one another?" Persons stand to one another as I, you, and they; a society forms them, binds them, and frees them. Its conceptual devices are first person-second person-third person and edification-piety-emancipation.

Its ground is sociality, the practice an enactive agent carries on among others. With language, sociality becomes heterosubjectivity, the encounter with other minds, and intersubjectivity, a world held in common.

History Note

  • Aristotle: the human being is by nature a political animal (Politics I.2, 1253a2).
  • Hegel: self-consciousness finds its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness; the struggle for recognition (Phenomenology of Spirit §§175–178, 1807).
  • Martin Buber sets the I–Thou relation beside the I–It (Ich und Du, 1923). Wikipedia: I and Thou
  • George Herbert Mead: the self arises in social experience (Mind, Self, and Society, 1934). Wikipedia: George Herbert Mead

Scope Note

SOCIETY is not sociology, and not its institutions: education, commerce, and jurisprudence belong to the Jurisdictions. No antinomy is at home here; the questions of SOCIETY are met in practice before they are puzzled over in theory.
