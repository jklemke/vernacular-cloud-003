Definition

Edification, piety, and emancipation are a conceptual device of SOCIETY: edification (Bildung) is how a society forms a person; piety is the reverence the person owes to what was inherited; emancipation is release from it. Formed, bound, freed: each is understood only against the other two.

History Note

  • Plato's Euthyphro asks what piety is and leaves the question open (c. 399 BCE).
  • Kant: enlightenment is the human being's emergence from self-incurred immaturity ("What Is Enlightenment?", 1784). Wikipedia: Sapere aude
  • Wilhelm von Humboldt's ideal of Bildung, the formation of the whole person (Theorie der Bildung des Menschen, c. 1793). Wikipedia: Bildung
  • Marx distinguishes political emancipation from human emancipation ("On the Jewish Question", 1843).

Scope Note

Edification is not education, the institutional Jurisdiction: it is formation, as in Bildung. Piety is not only religious: filial piety and the Roman pietas are piety too. Emancipation is release from an inherited bond, not the arrival at a haven.
