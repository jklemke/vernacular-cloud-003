Definition

The Myth of the Maven is the spell that falls on political practice when legitimacy is taken to reside in a person: the guru, the saviour, the bodhisattva, the prophet, the mystic, the strongman, the technocrat, the sophist, the great philosopher. The founders of religions, Moses, Jesus, the Buddha, Muhammad, have each been received as mavens.

Notes

Example

"Until philosophers are kings, or the kings and princes of this world have the spirit and power of philosophy … cities will never have rest from their evils."

Plato, Republic V, 473c–d (trans. Jowett)

History Note

  • Plato's philosopher-king (Rep. V, 473c–d); Protagoras and the sophists teach virtue for a fee (Prot. 318e–319a).
  • Kant defines enlightenment as release from self-incurred tutelage: "Have courage to use your own understanding" ("What Is Enlightenment?", 1784).
  • Thomas Carlyle holds that the history of the world is the biography of great men (On Heroes, 1841).
  • Max Weber describes charismatic authority, resting on devotion to the exceptional sanctity, heroism, or character of a person (Economy and Society, 1922). Wikipedia: Charismatic authority
  • Saint-Simon imagines society governed by scientists and industrialists, a forerunner of technocracy (1820s).

Scope Note

We need teachers, experts, and leaders; political practice is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Maven. The spell falls when authority is taken from a person rather than from a tribunal whose own legitimacy remains open to investigation. The philosopher-king is occult emancipation: the promise of freedom delivered by someone who knows better. The sage, the sophist who claims wisdom achieved, is a maven who has reached the haven.
