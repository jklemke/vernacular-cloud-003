Definition

Loyalty, empathy, and fairness are a conceptual device of MORALITY: three pulls on a rule, in widening circles. Loyalty is the pull of my own; empathy, the pull of the one in front of me; fairness, the pull of anyone at all.

They echo, quietly, the first, second, and third person. Each corrects the others: loyalty without fairness is partisanship, fairness without empathy is cold, and empathy without loyalty has no home.

History Note

  • Mozi's impartial care against the Confucian love graded by relationship. SEP: Mohism
  • Hume: sympathy, the communication of feeling from one person to another (Treatise 2.1.11, 1739).
  • Adam Smith's impartial spectator (The Theory of Moral Sentiments, 1759).
  • John Rawls: justice as fairness (A Theory of Justice, 1971).
  • Jonathan Haidt's moral foundations include care, fairness, and loyalty (The Righteous Mind, 2012). Wikipedia: Moral foundations theory

Scope Note

These are pulls on a rule, not the rule: MORALITY asks which rule I claim to act by. They are not moral foundations in Haidt's sense, though they overlap with his.
