Definition

A concept is the unit of signification at which a notion becomes linguistically articulated and intersubjectively shareable. Concept is a philosophical term of art that straddles the boundaries of language, psychology, culture, and reality. Concepts emerge through conceptualization: the intersubjective, transcendent act of assigning meanings to words and sentences.

Concepts are semantically rich complexes embedded in language. They take part in public norms of meaning, reference, and justification, while each participant in the language also interprets them idiosyncratically. A concept is like Frege's Sinn: a multiply realized meaning for a token, fixed in intersubjective space by the token grammar.

Notes

Example

"Sellars: Grasp of a concept is always mastery of the use of a word. Badinage: Grasp of a concept is always a conjectural use of a word."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Immanuel Kant: thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind (KrV A51/B75).
  • Gottlob Frege separates concept from object ("Über Begriff und Gegenstand", 1892) and sense from reference ("Über Sinn und Bedeutung", 1892). Wikipedia: Sense and reference
  • Wilfrid Sellars: to grasp a concept is to master the use of a word (Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind, 1956).
  • Eleanor Rosch: categories are organized around prototypes, not definitions ("Cognitive Representations of Semantic Categories", 1975). Wikipedia: Prototype theory

Scope Note

The concept emerges in the naming of the notion. Fail to see this and one confuses a notion in use as a recognitional template with a notion given a name.
