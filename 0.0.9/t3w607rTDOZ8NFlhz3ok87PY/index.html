Definition

Possible, impossible, and necessary are a conceptual device of CONTINGENCY: the possible is what could be actualized; the impossible is what could not; the necessary is what could not fail to be. Each is defined by the others: the necessary is what it is impossible not to be.

It is nearly Kant's Modality, with actuality left out, because actualization is what CONTINGENCY itself names. The possible is a capacity, a disposition, a power, a potential: not a world.

Example

It is possible that it rains tomorrow; impossible that it both rains and does not; necessary that it rains or does not.

History Note

  • Aristotle: the potential, dynamis, is what is capable of being actualized (Metaphysics Θ). SEP: Aristotle's Metaphysics
  • Diodorus Cronus's Master Argument: the possible is what is or will be true. SEP: Diodorus Cronus
  • Kant's categories of Modality: possibility–impossibility, existence–nonexistence, necessity–contingency (KrV A80/B106).
  • Leibniz's possible worlds (Theodicy, 1710); David Lewis's modal realism (On the Plurality of Worlds, 1986). SEP: Possible Worlds

Scope Note

Not a semantics of possible worlds, which Badinage holds to be an outlandish notion: Leibnizian rationalism in the costume of materialistic naturalism. Is everything an algorithm? Yes. Is there an algorithm for everything? No. The antinomy at home here is Absolute Necessity.
