Definition

The Myth of the Coven is the spell that falls on social practice when the group's agreement is taken for truth: truth as what your colleagues will let you get away with saying. Its forms are many: orthodoxy, dogmatism, identity politics, groupthink, fanaticism, nationalism, and, among the sophisticated, relativism.

Notes

Example

In Solomon Asch's experiments, people asked to match the length of a line agreed with a unanimous group's obviously wrong answer in about a third of the trials (1951).

"Social praxis is not discredited by calling it the Myth of the Coven."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Richard Rorty proposes that we see knowledge as "what our peers will, ceteris paribus, let us get away with saying" (Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature, 1979, 176).
  • Eric Hoffer's The True Believer (1951) studies the fanatic and the mass movement.
  • Solomon Asch measures conformity to a unanimous majority ("Effects of Group Pressure upon the Modification and Distortion of Judgments", 1951).
  • Irving Janis names "groupthink" (Victims of Groupthink, 1972). Wikipedia: Groupthink
  • Benedict Anderson describes the nation as an "imagined community" (Imagined Communities, 1983).

Scope Note

Social practice is corroboration among many sentient actors who observe one another's corroboration. The Coven is what that practice becomes when the circle closes and agreement replaces corroboration. In the Introduction the Myth of the Coven stands opposite the Myth of the Proven: the congregation against the calculation.
