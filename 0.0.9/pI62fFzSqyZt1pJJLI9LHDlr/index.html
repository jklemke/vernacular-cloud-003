Definition

Art is the jurisdiction in which the Norms are explored and manipulated. A work establishes metaphorical references to the interplay of all of them: survival, flourishing, reality, beauty, meaning, truth, and legitimacy.

Its tribunals are the critic, the gallery, the audience, and the canon, and none of their verdicts is final.

Example

"Beauty is truth, truth beauty."

John Keats, "Ode on a Grecian Urn" (1819)

History Note

  • Aristotle: tragedy is the imitation of an action that, through pity and fear, brings about catharsis (Poetics 6, 1449b24–28).
  • Kant: the beautiful pleases without a concept, and shows purposiveness without a purpose (Critique of Judgment §§10–17, 1790). SEP: Kant's Aesthetics
  • John Dewey: art as experience (Art as Experience, 1934).
  • Arthur Danto: to see something as art requires an artworld ("The Artworld", 1964).

Scope Note

Art is not the norm of beauty alone, nor decoration: it explores all the Norms, and their interplay. Its language is metaphor.
