Definition

ENTIRETY is the contested space of questions like "What is everything?" An analytical system based on the isolate and the aggregate cannot be made to apply to the totality, the absolute, the everything. Its conceptual devices are complete-enumerated-bounded and all-none-final.

The entirety is bounded, and the apeiron, the boundless, always lies outside the boundary. A Venn diagram pictures the entirety by drawing its edge and oubliating the apeiron. Hence the emptiness of "Why is there something rather than nothing?" and "What happened before the big bang?": each asks for a view from beyond the edge.

Example

The theory of everything is a logical contradiction: there cannot be a generalization that explains all particulars.

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Anaximander makes the apeiron, the boundless, the source of all things (DK 12 B1). Wikipedia: Apeiron
  • Plato's cosmos is a living creature with soul and intelligence (Timaeus 30b); Plotinos speaks of the Soul of the All (Enneads IV.3).
  • Leibniz: "Why is there something rather than nothing?" ("Principles of Nature and Grace", 1714, §7).
  • F. H. Bradley's Absolute (Appearance and Reality, 1893); Josiah Royce's Absolute is social and ethical (The World and the Individual, 1899–1901).
  • Quine: what is there? "Everything" ("On What There Is", 1948).

Scope Note

ENTIRETY is not the cosmos of cosmology nor the WORLD of a sentient being. It is not INFINITY either: the entirety is bounded, the boundless lies outside it. George Ellis: there is no wave function for the entire universe, only local ones. The antinomy at home in ENTIRETY is Absolute Description.
