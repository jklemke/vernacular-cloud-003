Definition

Percept, notion, and concept are a conceptual device of EPIPHANY: the percept is what is perceived; the notion is what the perceiver makes of it, purely internal to the IPSEITY; the concept is shared, presupposing IPSEITY, WORLD, SOCIETY, and LANGUAGE. The move from percept and notion to concept is the anagogical movement from a solitary world to an intersubjective one.

Philosophy struggles with how my thought engages with your word. The conceptualization boundary is a phase transition, into the intersubjective world of ostension, metaphor, and transcendence.

History Note

  • Kant: thoughts without content are empty, Anschauungen without concepts are blind (KrV A51/B75).
  • Frege: a concept is objective, not an idea in someone's mind (The Foundations of Arithmetic, Introduction, 1884).
  • Hilary Putnam: meanings just ain't in the head ("The Meaning of 'Meaning'", 1975). SEP: Externalism About the Mind

Scope Note

In the internalism and externalism debate, the notion is internal and the concept external. "Notion" also renders Kant's Anschauung (style guide §8, provisional): the move from notion to concept is Kant's move from Anschauung to concept, made social.
