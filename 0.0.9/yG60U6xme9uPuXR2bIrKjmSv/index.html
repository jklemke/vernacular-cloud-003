Definition

Beauty is the norm of aesthetic practice: what pleases in perception, and is sought, made, and judged for that reason. It is older than language. Birds, flowers, and fruit were shaped by it before anyone named it.

Broader

  • norm

Related

  • art

Notes

Example

"Beauty is truth, truth beauty,—that is all

Ye know on earth, and all ye need to know."

Keats, "Ode on a Grecian Urn" (1819)

A male bowerbird builds and decorates a bower of blue objects that has no use except to be judged by a female.

History Note

  • Plato's Diotima leads the lover up a ladder from one beautiful body to beauty itself (Symp. 210a–212a).
  • Kant finds in the judgment of taste a "purposiveness without purpose", universal yet not based on concepts (KU §§10–17). SEP: Kant's Aesthetics
  • Darwin explains ornaments such as the peacock's tail by sexual selection, the female's "taste for the beautiful" (Descent of Man, 1871).
  • Richard Prum revives Darwin's aesthetic selection: animal beauty can evolve for its own sake (The Evolution of Beauty, 2017).

Scope Note

Beauty is commonly described as a feature of objects that makes them pleasurable to perceive: landscapes, sunsets, people, works of art. As a norm it is the standard by which such perceiving and making is sorted, beautiful from ugly, better from worse. It is related to art but not confined to it.
