Definition

Flourishing is the norm of sentient practice: not only to stay alive but to thrive. It emerges from survival, and it sorts better from worse where survival sorts only alive from dead.

Broader

  • norm

Related

  • sentience
  • sociality

Notes

Alternative Label

  • thriving

Example

A houseplant in a dark corner survives for months, pale and leggy; moved to the window, it flourishes. A child who is fed but never played with survives; flourishing asks for more.

History Note

  • Aristotle makes eudaimonia, usually translated "happiness" or "flourishing", the highest human good, activity of the soul in accordance with virtue (NE I.7, 1098a16–18). SEP: Aristotle's Ethics
  • Philippa Foot grounds moral goodness in the natural goodness of living things: what a plant or animal needs to flourish as the kind of thing it is (Natural Goodness, 2001).
  • Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum measure human development by capabilities, what people are able to do and to be (Nussbaum, Creating Capabilities, 2011). Wikipedia: Capability approach

Scope Note

Flourishing belongs to every sentient creature, not only to the rational animal. Sociality emerges alongside it: for social creatures, to flourish is to flourish together.
