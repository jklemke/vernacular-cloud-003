Definition

Void is the ineffable notion of that which is not: absence, vacuum, negation, the unnoticed, the oubliated, the irrelevant. We use it in every "not", yet every attempt to say what nothing is makes it something.

Like each of the Unsayables, void cannot be defined; we must explicate it by use, in the prototype sentence: Subjectivity detects patterns of isolates, aggregates, and voids in time and space.

Notes

Example

Socratic wisdom is to know that I do not know (Plato, Ap. 21d).

Every "not" in everyday speech points at the void: the cup is not full, the train is not here.

History Note

  • Parmenides: what is not can be neither known nor spoken (DK 28B2). Plato's Sophist answers by treating not-being as difference (Soph. 257b).
  • Nāgārjuna teaches the emptiness (śūnyatā) of all things (Mūlamadhyamakakārikā).
  • Sartre makes nothingness the mark of consciousness (Being and Nothingness, 1943).
  • Terrence Deacon argues that life and mind emerge from constraints, and that intentionality is an absence with causal effect (Incomplete Nature, 2011).

Scope Note

Void is co-primitive with pattern: absence and negation are built in from the start, not derived later.
