Definition

Recept, aspect, and object are a conceptual device of IPSEITY: a recept is what the senses receive; an aspect is that reception interpreted as a property of something; an object is the something, a conjecture held for corroboration against the mind-independent world. All three are seen from the vantage of a subject, which is neither a recept nor an object.

The reception is immediate; the structure is mediated. Locke's "idea" and Frege's "sense" try to fit this structure into a single word. The result is neither direct nor indirect realism, but an asymmetric, aspectual realism: we see an aspect of reality, and subject and object are attached to the aspect.

History Note

  • Locke calls an idea whatever the mind perceives in itself, or is the immediate object of perception (Essay concerning Human Understanding II.viii.8, 1690).
  • Frege distinguishes the sense of a sign from its reference ("Über Sinn und Bedeutung", 1892). SEP: Gottlob Frege
  • Husserl: a thing is given only in adumbrations, one profile at a time (Ideas I §41, 1913).
  • Russell's knowledge by acquaintance with sense-data (The Problems of Philosophy ch. 5, 1912). SEP: Sense-Data
  • Bernard Baars's global workspace theory of consciousness (A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness, 1988). Wikipedia: Global workspace theory

Scope Note

This is not a sense-datum theory, nor representationalism: the object is a conjecture, and the subject an interpretive framework in which recepts, aspects, and objects become salient. The structure is the foundation of MDNMIW: a reception purports to be an aspect of an object, from this subjective vantage. Applied to three domains, it gives external-internal-central.
