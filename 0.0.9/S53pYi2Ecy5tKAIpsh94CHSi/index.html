Definition

Legitimacy is the norm of political practice: a tribunal, a court, a government, or a scientific community ought to deserve the authority it claims. Legitimacy co-evolves with culturally grounded social institutions, and every verdict of truth rests in the end on the legitimacy of the tribunal that renders it.

Notes

Example

The referee's call stands even when the replay shows it was wrong. The game goes on because the referee's authority is accepted, not because the referee is infallible; when the calls are bought, the game is over.

"A fact is a proposition which has been corroborated in a proceeding of a legitimate jurisdiction. Sentience is the ultimate legitimate jurisdiction."

Victor Badinage

History Note

  • Locke grounds political authority in the consent of the governed (Second Treatise §95).
  • Max Weber distinguishes three types of legitimate authority: traditional, charismatic, and legal-rational (Economy and Society, 1922). Wikipedia: Tripartite classification of authority
  • H. L. A. Hart finds the foundation of a legal system in a social "rule of recognition" (The Concept of Law, 1961).
  • Habermas diagnoses a "legitimation crisis" when a state can no longer secure the belief that its rule is justified (Legitimation Crisis, 1973). SEP: Political Legitimacy

Scope Note

Legitimacy is not mere stability or power. A tribunal is legitimate only so long as its own legitimacy remains open to investigation, and sentience, the encounter between organism and environment, remains the court of last appeal.
