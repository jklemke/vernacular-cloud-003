Definition

Event, system, and scenario are a conceptual device of WORLD: an event is a particular happening; a system is a functional-structural organization situated in space and time, an entropic macro-state that maintains its own boundary; a scenario is a possible course of events, one of the paths a system could move into.

A system is superveniently constrained by its ground and able to move into its possible paths. Its boundary is more than an observer's choice when sampling within it yields stable, correlated results that break down across the edge, and when that break persists over time. A flame, a whirlpool, a hurricane, a living cell: the more a system helps produce the constraints that define it, the more fully it is a system.

Wittgenstein's states of affairs are scenarios, not structures. Explanation here is modal: not that things are thus and so, but how they could go. Modern science forms deductive models in such a way that manipulating the model represents manipulating the world.

History Note

  • Wittgenstein: what is the case, a fact, is the existence of states of affairs (Tractatus 2, 1921).
  • Whitehead makes events, "actual occasions", the units of reality (Process and Reality, 1929). SEP: Alfred North Whitehead
  • Ludwig von Bertalanffy's general system theory (General System Theory, 1968). Wikipedia: Systems theory
  • Terrence Deacon: life begins with the closure of constraint production, a system that helps make the constraints that make it (Incomplete Nature, 2011). Wikipedia: Terrence Deacon
  • Pierre Wack's scenario planning at Shell ("Scenarios: Uncharted Waters Ahead", Harvard Business Review, 1985). Wikipedia: Scenario planning

Scope Note

The device is not a systems theory. It sits beside CONTINGENCY: a scenario is a possible actualization, an event an actual one, a system what constrains both. Situation, in the Grammar, specifies an event in time and space.
