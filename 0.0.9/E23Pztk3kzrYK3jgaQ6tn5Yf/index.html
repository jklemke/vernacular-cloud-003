Definition

Kinetics is an interpretive framework that explores the mind-independent world as patterns of motion: matter and energy changing in distance, duration, and energy. It is the framework of Newton and Einstein, and of how a quantum state evolves, and every other physical realm presupposes it.

Physics is the description of changes in inertia: how a changing boundary contributes to changes in an inertial system.

Example

Edmond Halley used Newton's laws to predict that the comet of 1682 would return late in 1758. He died in 1742. It was sighted on Christmas night, 1758.

History Note

  • Galileo founds the science of local motion (Two New Sciences, 1638). Wikipedia: Two New Sciences
  • Isaac Newton's three laws and universal gravitation (Principia, 1687). Wikipedia: Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica
  • Albert Einstein's special (1905) and general (1915) relativity. Wikipedia: General relativity
  • Heisenberg (1925) and Schrödinger (1926) give quantum mechanics its matrix and wave forms. Wikipedia: Quantum mechanics

Scope Note

Kinetics is not the discipline of physics: physics also works in the framework of molecularity (what a particle is made of), and astronomy, geology, physiology, and ecology all presuppose kinetics. Kinetics is not determinism. The general laws of physics set the boundary of the possible, but they do not determine which particular possibility will be actualized; there is no Absolute Perspective from which to calculate outcomes.
