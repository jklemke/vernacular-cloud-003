Definition

Jurisprudence is the operation of the tribunal: the institutionalization of suspension, a legitimate proceeding that assesses foundational norms and preconditions. Is the tribunal legitimate? Who makes the decisions? By what rules? According to what precedent?

Church courts, secular courts, boards of directors, and councils of elders are all tribunals. Its virtue is the disinterested judge; its corruption, the self-interested fraud. Behind it stand the monopoly on legitimate violence and the question of a just war.

History Note

  • Aeschylus makes Athena found the Areopagus, the court that ends the cycle of vengeance (Eumenides, 458 BCE).
  • Nemo iudex in causa sua: no one should be a judge in their own cause. Wikipedia: Nemo iudex in causa sua
  • Hugo Grotius: the law of war and peace (De jure belli ac pacis, 1625).
  • Milton's Areopagitica, against licensing the press (1644).
  • H. L. A. Hart: a legal system rests on a rule of recognition (The Concept of Law, 1961). SEP: Legal Positivism

Scope Note

Jurisprudence is the institution; MORALITY is the magisterium whose question it answers, "By what rule?" A tribunal is legitimate only so long as its judges are not judges in their own cause. The divine tribunal is the oubliation of justice on earth (see Absolute Justice).
