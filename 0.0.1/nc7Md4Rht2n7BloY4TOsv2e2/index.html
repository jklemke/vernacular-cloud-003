concept: corroboration

concept-key: https://vernacular.cloud/0.0.1/nc7Md4Rht2n7BloY4TOsv2e2/

Definition

Having a reliable method. Iterative, cumulative, corrective sampling of patterns in receptionality. The repeated application of the next step. The mechanism of recursion vis-a-vis a norm. That is, _subjectively indexed corroboration_.

Is justification internal or external. Compare to the black hole question. Can justification lose information? How did I learn this? Must I be able to retrace the steps to it? do we have access to the information that has given us these questions: is the soul immortal?

I am allowed to get a corroborational score from a legitimated institution? Revelation, peer-review, jurisprudence, experimental confirmation. Instrumentalism and engineering. What about apologetics, the performance of an argument?

Compare the internalist view of corroboration with the identity problems of Locke and Reid. Is this GWT again? How much access must we have to the reasons for our knowledge?

Pragmatism--rightly understood--is not a theory of truth. It is a theory about the legitimacy of corroboration. Pragmatism provides an escape from the quandary of certainty versus scepticism, but it leaves coherence, correspondence, and foundation in place as the norms or corroboration.

Corroboration Before Concepts: A Constraint-Based Account of Truth and Normativity

This paper develops a naturalistic account of epistemic normativity grounded in pre-linguistic forms of sentient practice. Against views that locate normativity exclusively within the conceptual or discursive domain—most prominently in the inferentialist tradition associated with Wilfrid Sellars, John McDowell, and Robert Brandom—I argue that norm-governed activity originates in embodied, self-correcting interaction between organism and environment. This interaction is already structured by what I call corroborational dynamics: patterns of success, failure, and adjustment that precede explicit judgment but exhibit proto-normative features.

On this view, linguistic and conceptual practices are not the source of normativity but higher-order stabilizations of these more basic dynamics. Epistemic judgment is therefore not grounded in a single criterion of truth but is situated within a field of constraints—foundation, correspondence, and coherence—which jointly govern both the production and evaluation of beliefs. These constraints are not competing theories of truth but dimensions of a unified practice of inquiry.

The account draws on and integrates several strands of contemporary research. From pragmatism, especially Charles Sanders Peirce and C. I. Lewis, it adopts a fallibilist conception of truth as the regulative norm of inquiry, while rejecting both ideal-limit metaphysics and Rortyan deflationism. From philosophy of science, it incorporates a Quinean holism, reinterpreted in “thermodynamic” terms as a system of belief revision constrained by costs, stability, and selective responsiveness. From cognitive science and psychology, it draws on enactive and predictive frameworks, as well as developmental work such as that of Alison Gopnik, to model cognition as an error-sensitive, model-building process that operates prior to and independently of linguistic articulation.

Within this framework, truth is understood as a norm governing practices of corroboration that unfold across multiple levels: (1) sentient interaction, where organisms achieve perceptual attunement and behavioral success; (2) sapient, discursive practice, where claims are articulated and assessed within structured vocabularies; and (3) institutional contexts, where socially organized “tribunals” (e.g., scientific communities, legal systems) stabilize and regulate standards of evaluation. Crucially, these tribunals are themselves subject to ongoing critical assessment, and their legitimacy depends on their responsiveness to the underlying constraints of organism–environment interaction.

The resulting position—describable as a form of pragmatically suspended corroborationalism—aims to reconcile naturalism with normativity without reducing one to the other. It offers a framework in which epistemic norms are neither imposed by language nor reducible to causal processes, but emerge within structured, corrigible practices that span biological, cognitive, and social domains.
