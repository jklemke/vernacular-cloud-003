concept: Myth of the Given

concept-key: https://vernacular.cloud/0.0.1/Eu3ily2TgNIEbilqIkdhQeWS/

Definition

In the first sentence of Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind, Wilfred Sellars equates the "given" with the "immediate":

I PRESUME that no philosopher who has attacked the philosophical idea of givenness or, to use the Hegelian term, immediacy has intended to deny that there is a difference between inferring that something is the case and, for example, seeing it to be the case.

!Victor Badinage Quotes by Theme#^120e54

We must remain vigilant in not confusing recognition with signification. That is, we must be wary of using sapient praxis as the model for sentient praxis. Myth of the Proven leading to Myth of the Given. But how does the perceptual relate to the actual? The perceptual is not an arbitrary signifier presented to an enduring subject. Neither is the perceptual a deterministic effect of some cause "out there". No, the perceptual is an immediate conjecture of similarity to past experience. The perceptual is a selection from received sensations, to which subjectivity and objectivity have been attached. There is a strand of thought (advocated by Wilfred Sellars and John McDowell) that wants to label this "similarity to past experience" as "conceptual". As Sellars say, "the ability to recognize that something looks green presupposes the concept of being green". The ability to recognize that something looks green does indeed presuppose a great deal about time and space and how colours relate to the external world. But an incantory invocation of the word "concept" will not suffice to explain all the intricacies of this relation. If the history of philosophy is an indication, the attempt to "explain" the intricacies of this relation will lead us into all sorts of antinomies. Now this leads us to quietism, also advocated by McDowell, following Wittgenstein.

JWK: Sellars is blurring the distinction between the physiology of sentience, and the rationality of sapience. What is "given" to consciousness is the conjectural object as indicated by its received aspects. (the "sense content" in Sellars' terminology). Sellars is still trapped in sense-datum theory, positing the "sense content" as an object of consciousness.

Sellars: the point of the epistemological category of the given is, presumably, to explicate the idea that empirical knowledge rests on a 'foundation' of non-inferential knowledge of matter of fact

JWK: we are arguing that empirical knowledge rests on a foundation of conjectural objects that result from "corroborational" practice. This practice is non-inferential in that it is non-verbal. Inferential corroboration is a grammaticalized practice that supervenes on corroborational sentient practice. What is known from sentient practice is neither "facts" nor "particulars"; what is "known" are recepts interpreted as aspects of a conjectural object (system).

Though Sellars takes the long way to his conclusion, his notion of seeming may be kin to our notion of the conjectural, corroborational object.

But our emphasis is on the corroborational practice. We especially want to distinguish between the corroborational practice of sentient engagement with the world and the corroborational practice of dreaming. !Victor Badinage Quotes by Theme#^b41cfd

Surely, Sellars notion of seeming is an attempt to get at the notion of corroboration. But Sellars falls into the Myth of the Proven by insisting the knowledge must be inferential, by which he means rational, the space of reasons. But Sellars has led us into the Labyrinth of Reasons, because he hasn't acknowledge the space of aspects. He is arguing against non-inferential knowledge, but he has missed the notion of corroborational knowledge.

Sellars: Now the idea that epistemic facts can be analyzed without remainder -- even "in principle " -- into non-epistemic facts, whether phenomenological or behavioral, public or private, with no matter how lavish a sprinkling of subjunctives and hypotheticals is, I believe, a radical mistake

JWK: what we're really talking about here is the misapplication of the analytical framework of molecularity. The question is this: are there non-epistemic atoms? Should we think of recepts as non-epistemic facts? No, they are selection from the patterns is space-time. Selected according to the interpretive template innate to sentient practice. !Victor Badinage Quotes by Theme#^bd1036
